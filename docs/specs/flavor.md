# flavor as-built specs

## FLAVOR-1 — Flavor test set and scorer, with today's baseline

data/flavor-truth.json: about 40 well-known cocktails, sauces and dishes that exist in the app's built-in data, each with the tastes a cook would expect (strongest first), tastes that must not lead, and aroma expectations only where uncontroversial. Written by the assistant from common culinary knowledge and marked as such in the file. scripts/measureFlavorProfile.mjs computes each item's chart exactly as the app does (same functions, same data files) and prints per item and overall: top taste is an expected one, top two both expected, a forbidden taste leads, share of ingredients counted; --json for machines. docs/flavor-profile.md records the method, the answer key's authorship and limits, and the baseline numbers for today's charts.

## FLAVOR-2 — Never draw a chart from placeholder values

Detect the constant vectors by value: a build step (in scripts/buildShareIndex.mjs or a sibling wired into the same prebuild) writes a small JSON listing every exact taste vector and aroma vector shared by more than a threshold number of ingredients in gnn_entropy.json. isPlaceholder(probs) uses it. Ingredients whose values are placeholder count as having no flavor data (they appear under 'Not counted'), separately for the taste half and the aroma half. A chart is drawn only when at least half of the ingredients, and at least two, have real values; otherwise the surface says there is not enough flavor data and draws nothing. Applies to buildFlavorProfile, recipeAxisProfile and recipeAxisProfileWeighted, so the Recipe Notebook, the invent panels, the shared page and both card renderers all follow; share-cards.json is regenerated. Boost / temper suggestions must not rank on placeholder values either.

## FLAVOR-3 — Tastes from the curated labels, weighted by amount

src/data/flavorTaste.js (pure): turns an ingredient's curated taste label from ingredients.json (one to three ordered words) into weights on six taste axes: sweet, sour, bitter, salty, umami, heat. First word 1.0, second 0.6, third 0.35; 'spicy' and 'pungent' both feed heat; 'astringent' counts half toward bitter. tasteProfile(lines, lookup) averages by gram-equivalent amount (reusing amountGrams), with equal weights when no amounts are known, and reports counted and uncounted. The taste labels are shipped to the pages that need them as a small file (not the full ingredients.json) so the shared page stays light. The model's taste values are no longer used for the taste half of any chart. After DATA-5 the curated taste labels use ten words: neutral (no taste of its own: water, starch, vodka) contributes to no taste axis but still counts as an ingredient with data, and rich (fat or creaminess) is a mouthfeel outside the six tastes and contributes to none of them, so for a label such as 'rich sweet' only the sweet counts, at its position's weight; see docs/flavor-profile.md, 'How neutral and rich are treated'. Herbs and warm spices: about 25 of them (parsley, thyme, oregano, basil, cumin, cinnamon, nutmeg, paprika, turmeric and others) carry 'pungent' as a second word meaning aromatic, which would chart as heat. Relabel those through tasteOverrides.json so pungent stays only on ingredients with real bite (garlic, raw onion, mustard, horseradish, ginger, pepper, chilies); each override with a reason.

## FLAVOR-4 — Hand-tuned strengths and potency for the ingredients that matter most

src/data/flavorStrengths.js: about 150 one-line entries covering base spirits, liqueurs, amari, vermouths and fortified wines, bitters, citrus and juices, sweeteners and syrups, sodas and mixers, dairy and eggs, sauce bases (stock, tomato, cream, butter, roux), salty and umami seasonings, chilies and hot sauces, and common herbs and spices. Each entry gives 0..3 on sweet, sour, bitter, salty, umami and heat, and a potency: how much a small amount counts (bitters, absinthe, hot sauce, fish sauce high; soda water and ice near zero). A table entry overrides the label-derived weights from FLAVOR-3. Keys go through the same normalisation as the bar and kitchen keys (barKey, kitchenKey) so 'campari', 'angostura bitters' and 'sweet vermouth' hit. Written by the assistant from common bartending and cooking knowledge and marked as such; the file header says how to correct a line.

## FLAVOR-5 — Aromas from published descriptors instead of the model

Confirm what gnn_compounds.json holds (how many ingredients, how many descriptor tags, the shape) and write that into docs/flavor-profile.md. A reviewed map from descriptor tags to the six aroma axes (fruity, floral, green, woody, spicy, fatty) in src/data/aromaDescriptorMap.js; tags with no home are listed and dropped. A build step writes a small per-ingredient aroma file (six numbers each, normalised) so pages do not load the full compound file. aromaProfile(lines, lookup) mirrors tasteProfile (amount weighting, counted and uncounted). Ingredients with no descriptors are not counted for aroma. The model's odor values are no longer used for any chart.

## FLAVOR-6 — One profile everywhere, honest wording, final measurement

A single profile function (tastes from FLAVOR-3 and FLAVOR-4, aromas from FLAVOR-5, the draw rule from FLAVOR-2) behind every chart: Recipe Notebook Flavor Profiles (overview bar chart, per-axis pages, boost / temper ranking via rankByAxisImpact), the three invent panels (InventFlavorProfile), the shared recipe page, and the share cards in the browser and on the server through the share index. The chart axes become the six tastes plus the six aromas; any axis list, colour or label that assumed five tastes is updated. Copy says the chart is built from taste labels and published aroma notes for each ingredient, weighted by amount, and is a guide rather than a taste test. Remove code paths that read the model's per-ingredient values for charts once nothing uses them (the radar views in the parked Network tab may keep them). .claude/.chemdataset-status.md gains a dated note pointing at docs/flavor-profile.md. Final scorer run recorded; three share cards re-rendered and looked at.
